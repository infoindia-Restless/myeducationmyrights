Why we start from rights, how the approach works, and how change holds from one girl to whole systems.

Why rights, why now

Education is a right, not a privilege

Article 21A of the Constitution of India makes education a fundamental right. For many girls, that right still stops at the school gate.

In tribal and marginalised communities, girls leave school before they can choose their future. Families move for seasonal work. Money runs short. Marriage comes early. Norms decide what a girl may study and where she may go.

We work so that young people do more than stay in school. They build the knowledge, confidence, skills and aspirations to pursue STEM and beyond, and the people and systems around them back that choice.

  • Poverty
  • Seasonal migration
  • Early marriage
  • Restrictive gender norms

Our approach

Learn. Lead. Link.

Every programme on the platform works at three levels at once: what a girl learns, how she leads, and who she is linked to.

01 · In school

Learn

Structured, in-school modules for girls in Classes 8 to 10.

  • STEM and Scratch coding
  • Social emotional learning
  • Gender and SRHR
  • Critical thinking and careers
02 · In the community

Lead

Youth Resource Centres as safe spaces, with Youth Facilitators and Youth Champions leading peer learning and campaigns.

03 · Into systems

Link

Connecting girls with families, schools, ITIs, colleges, employers and education authorities.

How change happens

From one girl to whole systems

Change has to hold at every level, or it does not last past Class 10.

  1. One girl

    She gains the knowledge, confidence and skills to stay in school and aim for STEM, and knows her rights.

  2. Her school and community

    Teachers, parents and peers back her learning, her safety and her ambitions.

  3. The systems around her

    Education authorities, ITIs, colleges and employers open pathways and are held to account for every girl's right to learn.
