My Education,
My Right

Odisha Ganjam

Adolescent girls from tribal and marginalised communities in Classes 8 to 10 stay in education and build the knowledge, confidence, skills and aspirations to pursue STEM and beyond.

A Restless Development India programme supported by Fondation L’Oréal through the L’Oréal Fund for Women. Phase 1, January 2023 to June 2024, was implemented by Student Partnership Worldwide India Project Trust; Phase 2, January 2025 to December 2026, is implemented by VIEWS. Results below are from Year 1 of Phase 2 (January to November 2025). The Year 2 endline is under way.

Learn · Lead · Link in Odisha

What girls did in Year 1

Classroom modules in every school, prototypes in the model schools, and links out to role models, workplaces and the community.

  1. Learn

    Modules in their own language

    Social emotional learning, gender and SRHR, coding and computational thinking, and careers, translated into the local language.

  2. Lead

    Ideathons and hackathons

    Model school groups designed prototypes for problems they chose, then presented them to teachers, headmasters and the Block Education Officer.

  3. Link

    Role models and workplaces

    Sessions with a pathologist, a forensic scientist, an economics professor and an education entrepreneur, and visits to a textile factory and an ITI.

Built by girls

Problems they chose, models they built

Students at Brundaban Govt. High School, Mother Sarojini Govt. Girls High School and Govt. Girls High School, Berhampur, brought 32 prototypes to two share outs. A selection:

  • Rain detector
  • Rainwater harvesting
  • Gas detector
  • Smart farming with solar energy
  • Anti flood house
  • Earthquake alarm
  • Girls' safety spray
  • Pad disposer
  • Herbal cleanser
  • School waste management
  • Water tank overflow alarm
  • Fire alarm system
  • Smart dustbin
  • QR code tree names
  • Maths module in Scratch

What we learned

What worked, and what we changed

  • Youth Facilitators became trusted, relatable bridges between school, home and community.
  • Prototype models gave students strong ownership of their learning.
  • Parent Teacher Meetings proved a key lever for changing norms.
  • ChallengeWeather, school closures and local events disrupted session schedules.
    What we changedFlexible scheduling protocols.
  • ChallengeVirtual mentorship depended on parents' smartphones, so many girls could not join consistently.
    What we changedIn person engagement prioritised in Year 2.
  • ChallengeSeasonal migration interrupts girls' participation.
    What we changedPhased attendance tracking and re-engagement plans.

What the programme leaves behind

My Education, My Right · Odisha closes in December 2026. These are the tools, people and relationships it has built, ready for schools, government and new partners to use.

  • Curriculum

    Classroom modules in the local language

    Social emotional learning, gender and SRHR, coding and computational thinking, and career education for Classes 8 to 10.

  • People

    Trained Youth Facilitators

    Six facilitators skilled in participatory sessions, coding, ideathons, hackathons and prototype making.

  • Tested models

    Three school prototypes

    Mentorship and role models, STEM clubs with project based learning, and STEM for social good, piloted in model schools.

  • Evidence

    A pre and post assessment

    A tested tool across STEM interest, critical thinking, perseverance and SRH knowledge, with Year 1 results for about 2,000 girls and a Year 2 endline under way.

  • Relationships

    Schools, officials and institutions

    18 schools chosen with the education department, block education officers, and links to the Govt. ITI in Berhampur.

  • Families

    Parents as allies

    Parent Teacher Meetings that proved a lever for changing norms around girls' education.

Who supports and delivers the programme

My Education, My Right · Odisha is a Restless Development India programme, supported by Fondation L’Oréal through the L’Oréal Fund for Women, and delivered on the ground by an implementing partner in each phase.

Supported by · Phases 1 and 2

Fondation L’Oréal

Through the L’Oréal Fund for Women, Fondation L’Oréal supported My Education, My Right · Odisha across both phases, backing adolescent girls in Ganjam to stay in school and pursue STEM. fondationloreal.com
